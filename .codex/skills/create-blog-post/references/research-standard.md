# Research Standard

## Window And Coverage

Use the last seven calendar days by default. Record the start and end dates in the source ledger. Search the requested topic plus all relevant lanes: model companies, robotics, research, and measured efficiency. Do not force one item from every lane when the evidence is weak.

## Source Hierarchy

1. Original release notes, official documentation, product pages, papers, benchmarks, datasets, regulatory filings, and first-party institutional posts.
2. Reputable independent reporting or analysis that adds material facts or context.
3. Social posts only as leads to a primary source, never as sole proof for a factual claim.

Open and read every primary source that supports a core claim. Verify the publication date from the page or a reliable accompanying source. Cite direct URLs in the article near the claim they support.

## Claim Discipline

- Say "the company reports" or "the paper reports" for self-published results.
- Name a benchmark's task, evaluation setting, and score before comparing it to another model.
- Separate a preview, limited rollout, research prototype, open-weight release, and generally available product.
- Treat an AI efficiency claim as usable only when its measurement states a workload, baseline, metric, and conditions. Report confounders such as human review, tool use, hardware, sample size, or evaluation horizon.
- Use cautious language for robotics: a successful demonstration does not establish reliability, safety, recovery behavior, or deployment readiness.
- Do not cite search-result snippets. Do not use copied news photos without clear permission or license.

## Source Ledger Format

Keep this with the draft or in the work folder. It is editorial evidence, not necessarily public copy.

| Claim | Event date | Primary source | Corroboration | Confidence | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provider released model X | YYYY-MM-DD | URL | URL or n/a | High | Availability or benchmark scope |

Reject duplicate stories, stale announcements outside the window, and claims that cannot be stated accurately in one sentence.
